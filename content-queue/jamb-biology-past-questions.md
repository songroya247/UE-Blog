<h2>Comprehensive JAMB Biology Past Questions by Module</h2>

<p>Below is a curated compilation of standard JAMB Biology past questions, organized by module. Each entry includes four option choices, the correct answer, and an in-depth explanation detailing why the correct option stands and why other choices are incorrect.</p>

<h3>Module 1: Cell Biology & Organization</h3>

<p><strong>Question 1:</strong> Which organelle is primarily responsible for synthesizing ATP through aerobic cellular respiration in eukaryotic cells?</p>
<ul>
<li>A. Ribosome</li>
<li>B. Mitochondrion</li>
<li>C. Golgi body</li>
<li>D. Endoplasmic reticulum</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B. Mitochondrion</strong></p>
<p><em>Explanation:</em> Mitochondria serve as the energy powerhouses of eukaryotic cells. They possess double-membrane structures where the Krebs cycle and electron transport chain take place, generating adenosine triphosphate (ATP) through aerobic respiration. According to structural biological research archived on the <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/">National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI)</a>, the inner mitochondrial membrane forms cristae to maximize surface area for ATP synthase enzymes. Ribosomes (Option A) function exclusively in protein synthesis. The Golgi body (Option C) packages and modifies proteins for secretion. The endoplasmic reticulum (Option D) synthesizes lipids and facilitates intracellular transport.</p>

<p><strong>Question 2:</strong> A plant cell immersed in a hypertonic solution undergoes shrinkage of its cytoplasm away from the cell wall. This physiological phenomenon is termed:</p>
<ul>
<li>A. Hemolysis</li>
<li>B. Plasmolysis</li>
<li>C. Turgidity</li>
<li>D. Deplasmolysis</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B. Plasmolysis</strong></p>
<p><em>Explanation:</em> When a plant cell is placed in a hypertonic environment (where the external solute concentration exceeds the internal cytosolic concentration), water leaves the cell via exosmosis. This loss of turgor pressure causes the plasma membrane and cytoplasm to pull away from the rigid cell wall, a process known as plasmolysis. Hemolysis (Option A) refers specifically to the bursting of red blood cells in hypotonic solutions. Turgidity (Option C) occurs when a plant cell absorbs water in a hypotonic medium, swelling against its cell wall. Deplasmolysis (Option D) is the reversal of plasmolysis when the cell is placed back into a hypoosmotic fluid.</p>

<p><strong>Question 3:</strong> During which stage of mitotic cell division do sister chromatids separate and move toward opposite poles of the spindle apparatus?</p>
<ul>
<li>A. Prophase</li>
<li>B. Metaphase</li>
<li>C. Anaphase</li>
<li>D. Telophase</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: C. Anaphase</strong></p>
<p><em>Explanation:</em> Anaphase is characterized by the cleavage of centromeres, allowing motor proteins to pull sister chromatids apart toward opposite poles of the dividing cell. Prophase (Option A) involves chromatin condensation and nuclear envelope breakdown. Metaphase (Option B) aligns chromosomes along the equatorial plate. Telophase (Option D) involves nuclear envelope reformation around separated chromatin sets.</p>

<div class="callout tip">
<strong class="label">Pro Tip: Cell Biology Questions</strong>
Examiners frequently test organelle functions by asking candidates to identify structural adaptations. Always pair organelle names with their primary biochemical pathway (e.g., Chloroplast $\rightarrow$ Photosynthesis, Lysosome $\rightarrow$ Intracellular Digestion).
</div>

<h3>Module 2: Genetics, Heredity & Variation</h3>

<p><strong>Question 4:</strong> In a monohybrid cross between two heterozygous tall pea plants ($Tt$), what is the expected phenotypic ratio of the offspring?</p>
<ul>
<li>A. 1 Tall : 3 Short</li>
<li>B. 3 Tall : 1 Short</li>
<li>C. 1 Tall : 2 Medium : 1 Short</li>
<li>D. 4 Tall : 0 Short</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B. 3 Tall : 1 Short</strong></p>
<p><em>Explanation:</em> Crossing two heterozygous individuals ($Tt \times Tt$) generates genotypes $1\ TT$, $2\ Tt$, and $1\ tt$. Because the tall allele ($T$) is completely dominant over the dwarf allele ($t$), both $TT$ and $Tt$ genotypes display the tall phenotype. This produces a phenotypic ratio of 3 tall plants to 1 short plant (75% dominant, 25% recessive). Standard genetic models maintained by <a href="https://www.nature.com/scitable/">Nature Education Scitable</a> confirm this basic Mendelian ratio. Option C represents a 1:2:1 genotypic ratio, not a phenotypic ratio.</p>

<p><strong>Question 5:</strong> Hemophilia is an X-linked recessive disorder. If a normal man marries a carrier woman ($X^H X^h$), what proportion of their male children will inherit hemophilia?</p>
<ul>
<li>A. 0%</li>
<li>B. 25%</li>
<li>C. 50%</li>
<li>D. 100%</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: C. 50%</strong></p>
<p><em>Explanation:</em> Males inherit their single X chromosome from their mother and their Y chromosome from their father ($XY$). The carrier mother possesses one normal allele ($X^H$) and one mutant allele ($X^h$). Each male child has a 50% chance of receiving the $X^h$ chromosome from the mother, resulting in hemophilia ($X^h Y$). Among all offspring combined, the probability is 25%, but specifically among male children, the probability is 50%.</p>

<p><strong>Question 6:</strong> Which human blood group genotype demonstrates the genetic principle of codominance?</p>
<ul>
<li>A. $I^A I^A$</li>
<li>B. $I^B i$</li>
<li>C. $I^A I^B$</li>
<li>D. $i i$</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: C. $I^A I^B$</strong></p>
<p><em>Explanation:</em> Codominance occurs when both alleles in a heterozygous individual are fully expressed without blending. In the ABO blood system, allele $I^A$ directs the production of A-antigens and $I^B$ directs B-antigens. An individual with genotype $I^A I^B$ displays both A and B antigens on red blood cell surfaces, resulting in Type AB blood. Genotypes $I^A I^A$ (Option A) and $i i$ (Option D) represent homozygous conditions, while $I^B i$ (Option B) demonstrates complete dominance of $I^B$ over $i$.</p>

<div class="callout tip">
<strong class="label">Pro Tip: Decoding Genetics Problems</strong>
Pay close attention to whether a question asks for offspring proportions relative to *all children* or *specifically among male/female offspring*. Misreading this filter is one of the most common reasons candidates lose marks in genetics calculations.
</div>

<h3>Module 3: Ecology & Environmental Biology</h3>

<p><strong>Question 7:</strong> In a terrestrial ecosystem, what proportion of energy captured at one trophic level is typically transferred to the next higher trophic level?</p>
<ul>
<li>A. 1%</li>
<li>B. 10%</li>
<li>C. 50%</li>
<li>D. 90%</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B. 10%</strong></p>
<p><em>Explanation:</em> According to Lindeman's Efficiency Law in ecological energetics, approximately 10% of energy stored as biomass in one trophic level gets passed to the next. The remaining 90% is dissipated as metabolic heat during cellular respiration, excretion, and unconsumed organic matter. Option D represents the energy *lost*, whereas Option B accurately represents the energy *transferred*.</p>

<p><strong>Question 8:</strong> The soil bacterium responsible for converting nitrites ($NO_2^-$) into nitrates ($NO_3^-$) during the nitrogen cycle is:</p>
<ul>
<li>A. <em>Azotobacter</em></li>
<li>B. <em>Nitrosomonas</em></li>
<li>C. <em>Nitrobacter</em></li>
<li>D. <em>Rhizobium</em></li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: C. <em>Nitrobacter</em></strong></p>
<p><em>Explanation:</em> Nitrification occurs in two distinct chemoautotrophic steps. First, <em>Nitrosomonas</em> (Option B) oxidizes ammonia ($NH_3$) into nitrites ($NO_2^-$). Second, <em>Nitrobacter</em> (Option C) oxidizes nitrites into nitrates ($NO_3^-$), which plants absorb. <em>Azotobacter</em> (Option A) is a free-living nitrogen-fixing bacterium, while <em>Rhizobium</em> (Option D) is a symbiotic nitrogen-fixing bacterium living in legume root nodules.</p>

<p><strong>Question 9:</strong> The primary biotic community that establishes itself on a newly exposed, bare rocky substrate is referred to as the:</p>
<ul>
<li>A. Climax community</li>
<li>B. Seral community</li>
<li>C. Pioneer community</li>
<li>D. Transitional community</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: C. Pioneer community</strong></p>
<p><em>Explanation:</em> Primary ecological succession begins on uncolonized land surfaces devoid of soil (such as bare rock or cooled lava). Crustose lichens and mosses serve as pioneer organisms because they can survive harsh, nutrient-poor conditions and secrete organic acids that weather rock into soil. The final stable, self-sustaining ecosystem is the climax community (Option A).</p>

<div class="callout tip">
<strong class="label">Pro Tip: Ecological Relationships</strong>
Ensure you can distinguish between primary succession (starts on bare rock with no pre-existing soil) and secondary succession (starts after a disturbance, such as a forest fire, where soil remains intact).
</div>

<h3>Module 4: Plant & Animal Physiology</h3>

<p><strong>Question 10:</strong> During the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis, the primary source of oxygen gas released into the atmosphere is:</p>
<ul>
<li>A. Carbon dioxide ($CO_2$)</li>
<li>B. Water ($H_2 O$)</li>
<li>C. Glucose ($C_6 H_{12} O_6$)</li>
<li>D. Atmospheric nitrogen ($N_2$)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B. Water ($H_2 O$)</strong></p>
<p><em>Explanation:</em> Photolysis occurs within the thylakoid lumen inside chloroplasts, where light energy splits water molecules ($2H_2 O \rightarrow 4H^+ + 4e^- + O_2$). The released oxygen diffuses out through stomata as a byproduct. Heavy isotope tracing experiments prove that oxygen originates entirely from water molecules, not from carbon dioxide reduction (Option A).</p>

<p><strong>Question 11:</strong> Which blood vessel carries deoxygenated blood directly from the right ventricle of the mammalian heart to the lungs?</p>
<ul>
<li>A. Pulmonary vein</li>
<li>B. Aorta</li>
<li>C. Pulmonary artery</li>
<li>D. Vena cava</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: C. Pulmonary artery</strong></p>
<p><em>Explanation:</em> Arteries carry blood away from the heart. The pulmonary artery is the only artery in the adult mammalian body that carries deoxygenated blood, transporting it from the right ventricle to the lungs for gaseous exchange. The pulmonary vein (Option A) returns oxygenated blood from the lungs to the left atrium. The aorta (Option B) distributes oxygenated blood to systemic tissues, while the vena cava (Option D) collects deoxygenated blood from the body into the right atrium.</p>

<p><strong>Question 12:</strong> In the human nephron, selective reabsorption of glucose, amino acids, and essential inorganic salts occurs primarily in the:</p>
<ul>
<li>A. Glomerulus</li>
<li>B. Bowman's capsule</li>
<li>C. Proximal convoluted tubule</li>
<li>D. Collecting duct</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: C. Proximal convoluted tubule</strong></p>
<p><em>Explanation:</em> The proximal convoluted tubule (PCT) contains epithelial cells packed with microvilli and mitochondria to drive active transport. Approximately 65% of filtered water, sodium, and 100% of essential nutrients (glucose and amino acids) are reabsorbed from the renal filtrate back into peritubular capillaries here. The glomerulus and Bowman's capsule (Options A and B) carry out ultrafiltration, not selective reabsorption.</p>

<div class="callout tip">
<strong class="label">Pro Tip: Mammalian Systems</strong>
Always map direction of flow alongside oxygenation state. Arteries leave the heart; veins enter the heart. Pulmonary circulation reverses the usual rule: pulmonary arteries carry deoxygenated blood, and pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood.
</div>

<h3>Module 5: Evolution & Biodiversity</h3>

<p><strong>Question 13:</strong> Pentadactyl limbs found in humans, bats, whales, and birds demonstrate structural similarity despite serving different locomotion functions. This provides anatomical evidence for:</p>
<ul>
<li>A. Analogous structures through convergent evolution</li>
<li>B. Homologous structures through divergent evolution</li>
<li>C. Vestigial organs through disuse</li>
<li>D. Parallel mutations</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B. Homologous structures through divergent evolution</strong></p>
<p><em>Explanation:</em> Homologous structures share a common ancestral skeletal plan (e.g., humerus, radius, ulna, carpals, metacarpals), but have modified over time to perform diverse functions such as grasping, flying, swimming, or running. This structural unity indicates divergent evolution from a shared vertebrate ancestor. Analogous structures (Option A), like insect wings and bird wings, perform similar functions but lack a common anatomical origin.</p>

<p><strong>Question 14:</strong> Which evolutionary scientist proposed the principle of "Use and Disuse" alongside the inheritance of acquired characteristics?</p>
<ul>
<li>A. Charles Darwin</li>
<li>B. Gregor Mendel</li>
<li>C. Jean-Baptiste Lamarck</li>
<li>D. Thomas Malthus</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: C. Jean-Baptiste Lamarck</strong></p>
<p><em>Explanation:</em> Jean-Baptiste Lamarck hypothesized that organisms develop or lose structural adaptations during their lifetimes based on environmental demands, and then pass those modified traits directly to offspring. While modern genetics disproved this mechanism in favor of Darwinian natural selection acting on genetic variation (Option A), questions regarding Lamarckian theory regularly appear in UTME exams.</p>

<p><strong>Question 15:</strong> Arrange the major taxonomic ranks in correct descending order of hierarchy:</p>
<ul>
<li>A. Kingdom $\rightarrow$ Class $\rightarrow$ Phylum $\rightarrow$ Order $\rightarrow$ Family $\rightarrow$ Genus $\rightarrow$ Species</li>
<li>B. Kingdom $\rightarrow$ Phylum $\rightarrow$ Class $\rightarrow$ Order $\rightarrow$ Family $\rightarrow$ Genus $\rightarrow$ Species</li>
<li>C. Kingdom $\rightarrow$ Order $\rightarrow$ Phylum $\rightarrow$ Class $\rightarrow$ Genus $\rightarrow$ Family $\rightarrow$ Species</li>
<li>D. Phylum $\rightarrow$ Kingdom $\rightarrow$ Class $\rightarrow$ Order $\rightarrow$ Family $\rightarrow$ Genus $\rightarrow$ Species</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B. Kingdom $\rightarrow$ Phylum $\rightarrow$ Class $\rightarrow$ Order $\rightarrow$ Family $\rightarrow$ Genus $\rightarrow$ Species</strong></p>
<p><em>Explanation:</em> Standard Linnaean classification organizes living organisms into hierarchical ranks. The broad descending sequence flows from Domain to Kingdom, Phylum (or Division in botany), Class, Order, Family, Genus, and finally individual Species. Use the mnemonic <em>"King Philip Came Over For Good Soup"</em> to remember this sequence during the exam.</p>

<div class="callout tip">
<strong class="label">Pro Tip: Evolutionary Concepts</strong>
Distinguish clearly between evidence from comparative anatomy (homology vs analogy) and evidence from paleontology (fossil sequences). Examiners routinely test this distinction using bird, bat, and insect wing comparisons.
</div>

<h3>Module 6: Applied Biology & Biotechnology</h3>

<p><strong>Question 16:</strong> Small, circular, double-stranded DNA molecules found in bacterial cells that are widely used as vectors in recombinant DNA technology are called:</p>
<ul>
<li>A. Centromeres</li>
<li>B. Plasmids</li>
<li>C. Bacteriophages</li>
<li>D. Introns</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B. Plasmids</strong></p>
<p><em>Explanation:</em> Plasmids exist naturally in bacteria independently of chromosomal DNA. Molecular biologists cut plasmids using restriction endonucleases to insert foreign target genes (such as the human insulin gene). The recombinant plasmid is then reintroduced into host bacteria for gene cloning and protein expression. Bacteriophages (Option C) are viruses that infect bacteria, while introns (Option D) represent non-coding regions within eukaryotic genes.</p>

<p><strong>Question 17:</strong> The introduction of ladybird beetles to control aphid infestations in crops serves as an agricultural application of:</p>
<ul>
<li>A. Chemical pest control</li>
<li>B. Biological pest control</li>
<li>C. Eutrophication</li>
<li>D. Genetic mutagenesis</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B. Biological pest control</strong></p>
<p><em>Explanation:</em> Biological control utilizes natural predators, parasites, or pathogens to reduce pest populations below economically damaging levels without toxic chemicals. Using ladybird beetles to prey on aphids eliminates environmental hazards, chemical runoff, and pesticide resistance issues associated with synthetic insecticides (Option A).</p>

<p><strong>Question 18:</strong> In industrial brewing, the anaerobic breakdown of glucose by yeast cells ($Saccharomyces\ cerevisiae$) yields ethanol alongside which gaseous byproduct?</p>
<ul>
<li>A. Oxygen ($O_2$)</li>
<li>B. Carbon dioxide ($CO_2$)</li>
<li>C. Methane ($CH_4$)</li>
<li>D. Nitrogen dioxide ($NO_2$)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B. Carbon dioxide ($CO_2$)</strong></p>
<p><em>Explanation:</em> Alcoholic fermentation converts simple sugars into ethanol and carbon dioxide under anaerobic conditions ($C_6 H_{12} O_6 \rightarrow 2C_2 H_5 OH + 2CO_2 + 2\text{ ATP}$). The produced $CO_2$ gas causes bread dough to rise in baking and provides carbonation in commercial beverages.</p>

<div class="callout tip">
<strong class="label">Pro Tip: Applied Biology Principles</strong>
Applied biology questions focus on practical real-world outcomes. Link industrial processes directly to their governing organism and metabolic pathway (e.g., Yeast $\rightarrow$ Fermentation $\rightarrow$ Ethanol + $CO_2$).
</div>

<section id="strategic-answering-guide">
<h2>Strategic Answering Guide & Trap Avoidance</h2>

<p>Scoring high on the UTME Biology CBT requires disciplined execution during the examination. Avoiding common exam traps is just as crucial as knowing the biological facts.</p>

<div class="status-rail">
<div class="status-step">
<span class="tag">Stage 01</span>
<h4>Read Question Stems Carefully</h4>
<p>Identify critical filter words like <strong>NOT</strong>, <strong>EXCEPT</strong>, <strong>INCORRECT</strong>, or <strong>PRIMARY</strong>. Many candidates lose points by selecting a true statement when the stem specifically asked for the false option.</p>
</div>
<div class="status-step">
<span class="tag">Stage 02</span>
<h4>Systematic Option Elimination</h4>
<p>Eliminate two obviously incorrect distractors immediately. This improves your odds to 50% even when facing difficult topics, making your final selection much easier.</p>
</div>
<div class="status-step">
<span class="tag">Stage 03</span>
<h4>Diagram & Data Verification</h4>
<p>When reviewing diagrams, read the question stem before looking at the visual. Verify pointer arrows, axis units, and labels carefully before selecting your choice.</p>
</div>
<div class="status-step negative">
<span class="tag">Flagged</span>
<h4>Avoid Blind Time Wasting</h4>
<p>Do not spend more than 60 seconds on any single question. If a question seems confusing, pick your best estimate, mark it for review on the CBT screen, and move forward.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="callout warn">
<strong class="label">Important Rule: Absolute Distractors</strong>
Be cautious with options containing absolute words such as <em>"always," "never," "all,"</em> or <em>"entirely."</em> Biological systems usually involve exceptions. Options with moderated phrasing are statistically more likely to be correct.
</div>
</section>
