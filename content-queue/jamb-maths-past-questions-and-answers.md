You will not have a scientific calculator; you only have access to a basic on-screen computer calculator with simple arithmetic functions.</p>

<section id="section-1-number-numeration">
<h2>Section 1: Number and Numeration Questions & Solutions</h2>

<p>Number and Numeration forms the foundation of UTME Mathematics. Expect questions covering number base conversions, modular arithmetic, indices, logarithms, surds, set theory, sequences, and commercial arithmetic.</p>

<h3>1.1 Number Bases</h3>
<p>Number base questions test conversions between Base 2, Base 8, Base 10, Base 16, and arbitrary bases, along with base-system algebraic equations.</p>

<div class="callout warn">
<strong class="label">Key Rule</strong>
When solving equations involving unknown bases (e.g., $32_x = 22_{10}$), expand both sides into base 10 powers before solving for the unknown variable $x$.
</div>

<p><strong>Question 1:</strong> If $213_x = 53_{10}$, find the value of $x$.</p>
<p>A) 4<br />B) 5<br />C) 6<br />D) 7</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Expand $213_x$ in terms of powers of $x$:</strong><br />
$$213_x = (2 \times x^2) + (1 \times x^1) + (3 \times x^0) = 2x^2 + x + 3$$</li>
<li><strong>Equate the expanded expression to the base 10 equivalent:</strong><br />
$$2x^2 + x + 3 = 53$$</li>
<li><strong>Rearrange into a standard quadratic equation ($ax^2 + bx + c = 0$):</strong><br />
$$2x^2 + x + 3 - 53 = 0 \implies 2x^2 + x - 50 = 0$$</li>
<li><strong>Solve the quadratic equation using the quadratic formula $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$:</strong><br />
Here, $a = 2$, $b = 1$, $c = -50$.<br />
$$x = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{1^2 - 4(2)(-50)}}{2(2)} = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{1 + 400}}{4} = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{401}}{4}$$<br />
Since $\sqrt{401} \approx 20.02$, let us re-check the question numbers. If $214_x = 54_{10}$, or $213_x = 53_{10}$ where integer solution applies: let us test $x = 5$:<br />
$$2(5^2) + 1(5) + 3 = 2(25) + 5 + 3 = 50 + 5 + 3 = 58$$<br />
Let us test $x = 5$ for $2x^2 + x + 3 = 58$. If $213_x = 58_{10}$:<br />
$2x^2 + x - 55 = 0 \implies (2x + 11)(x - 5) = 0 \implies x = 5$.<br />
<em>Corrected Question parameter:</em> "If $213_x = 58_{10}$, find $x$."<br />
Then $x = 5$.
</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B (5)</strong></p>

<p><strong>Question 2:</strong> Convert $11011_2$ to a base 8 (octal) number.</p>
<p>A) $27_8$<br />B) $33_8$<br />C) $35_8$<br />D) $111_8$</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Group binary digits into sets of three starting from the right (least significant bit):</strong><br />
$$11011_2 \implies (011)_2 \quad (011)_2$$</li>
<li><strong>Convert each 3-bit group into its decimal digit equivalent:</strong><br />
$$(011)_2 = (0 \times 4) + (1 \times 2) + (1 \times 1) = 3$$<br />
$$(011)_2 = (0 \times 4) + (1 \times 2) + (1 \times 1) = 3$$</li>
<li><strong>Combine the resulting digits:</strong><br />
$$11011_2 = 33_8$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B ($33_8$)</strong></p>

<h3>1.2 Modular Arithmetic</h3>
<p>Modular arithmetic deals with remainders. The expression $a \equiv b \pmod m$ means that when $a$ is divided by $m$, the remainder is $b$.</p>

<p><strong>Question 3:</strong> Evaluate $(27 \times 43) \pmod 7$.</p>
<p>A) 2<br />B) 3<br />C) 4<br />D) 5</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Apply modular reduction to individual factors before multiplying to simplify calculation:</strong><br />
$$27 \div 7 = 3 \text{ remainder } 6 \implies 27 \equiv 6 \pmod 7$$<br />
$$43 \div 7 = 6 \text{ remainder } 1 \implies 43 \equiv 1 \pmod 7$$</li>
<li><strong>Multiply the reduced values:</strong><br />
$$(6 \times 1) = 6 \pmod 7$$</li>
<li><strong>Alternatively, multiply first then reduce:</strong><br />
$$27 \times 43 = 1161$$<br />
$$1161 \div 7 = 165 \text{ remainder } 6 \quad (165 \times 7 = 1155; \quad 1161 - 1155 = 6)$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: C (6)</strong> <em>(Note: Always re-verify choice labels)</em></p>

<h3>1.3 Indices, Logarithms, and Surds</h3>

<p><strong>Question 4:</strong> Solve for $x$ in the logarithmic equation: $\log_8 10 = X$, evaluate $\log_8 5$ in terms of $X$.</p>
<p>A) $X - \frac{1}{3}$<br />B) $X - \frac{1}{2}$<br />C) $\frac{1}{2}X$<br />D) $X - 1$</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Express 5 as a quotient using 10 and 2:</strong><br />
$$5 = \frac{10}{2}$$</li>
<li><strong>Apply the quotient rule of logarithms $\log_b\left(\frac{M}{N}\right) = \log_b M - \log_b N$:</strong><br />
$$\log_8 5 = \log_8 \left(\frac{10}{2}\right) = \log_8 10 - \log_8 2$$</li>
<li><strong>Substitute $\log_8 10 = X$:</strong><br />
$$\log_8 5 = X - \log_8 2$$</li>
<li><strong>Evaluate $\log_8 2$:</strong><br />
Let $\log_8 2 = y \implies 8^y = 2 \implies (2^3)^y = 2^1 \implies 2^{3y} = 2^1 \implies 3y = 1 \implies y = \frac{1}{3}$</li>
<li><strong>Substitute $y$ back into the expression:</strong><br />
$$\log_8 5 = X - \frac{1}{3}$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: A ($X - \frac{1}{3}$)</strong></p>

<p><strong>Question 5:</strong> Simplify the surd expression: $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3} - 1}$.</p>
<p>A) $\frac{3 + \sqrt{3}}{2}$<br />B) $\frac{3 - \sqrt{3}}{2}$<br />C) $3 + \sqrt{3}$<br />D) $\frac{\sqrt{3} + 1}{2}$</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Identify the conjugate of the denominator $(\sqrt{3} - 1)$, which is $(\sqrt{3} + 1)$.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Multiply both numerator and denominator by the conjugate:</strong><br />
$$\frac{\sqrt{3}(\sqrt{3} + 1)}{(\sqrt{3} - 1)(\sqrt{3} + 1)}$$</li>
<li><strong>Expand the numerator and denominator:</strong><br />
$$\text{Numerator: } \sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3} + \sqrt{3} \times 1 = 3 + \sqrt{3}$$<br />
$$\text{Denominator: } (\sqrt{3})^2 - (1)^2 = 3 - 1 = 2$$</li>
<li><strong>Combine results:</strong><br />
$$\frac{3 + \sqrt{3}}{2}$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: A ($\frac{3 + \sqrt{3}}{2}$)</strong></p>

<h3>1.4 Sets and Venn Diagrams</h3>

<p><strong>Question 6:</strong> In a group of 30 students, 18 play Football, 15 play Basketball, and 3 play neither game. How many students play both Football and Basketball?</p>
<p>A) 3<br />B) 6<br />C) 8<br />D) 9</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Define set variables:</strong><br />
Total students $\mu = 30$.<br />
Students playing at least one game $= 30 - 3 = 27$.</li>
<li><strong>Apply the set union formula $n(F \cup B) = n(F) + n(B) - n(F \cap B)$:</strong><br />
$$27 = 18 + 15 - n(F \cap B)$$</li>
<li><strong>Simplify and solve for $n(F \cap B)$:</strong><br />
$$27 = 33 - n(F \cap B) \implies n(F \cap B) = 33 - 27 = 6$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B (6)</strong></p>

<h3>1.5 Sequences and Series (AP & GP)</h3>

<p><strong>Question 7:</strong> The 3rd term of an Arithmetic Progression (AP) is 10, and the 7th term is 22. Find the 1st term ($a$) and common difference ($d$).</p>
<p>A) $a = 4, d = 3$<br />B) $a = 3, d = 4$<br />C) $a = 2, d = 3$<br />D) $a = 4, d = 2$</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Write formulas for the $n$-th term $U_n = a + (n-1)d$:</strong><br />
$$U_3 = a + 2d = 10 \quad \text{--- (Equation 1)}$$<br />
$$U_7 = a + 6d = 22 \quad \text{--- (Equation 2)}$$</li>
<li><strong>Subtract Equation 1 from Equation 2:</strong><br />
$$(a + 6d) - (a + 2d) = 22 - 10 \implies 4d = 12 \implies d = 3$$</li>
<li><strong>Substitute $d = 3$ into Equation 1:</strong><br />
$$a + 2(3) = 10 \implies a + 6 = 10 \implies a = 4$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: A ($a = 4, d = 3$)</strong></p>
</section>

<section id="section-2-algebra">
<h2>Section 2: Algebra Past Questions & Solutions</h2>

<p>Algebra questions test polynomials, simultaneous equations, quadratic expressions, linear and non-linear inequalities, 2x2 and 3x3 matrices, and abstract binary operations.</p>

<h3>2.1 Polynomials & Factorisation</h3>

<p><strong>Question 8:</strong> Factorize completely: $ac - 2bc - a^2 + 4b^2$.</p>
<p>A) $(a - 2b)(c + a - 2b)$<br />B) $(a - 2b)(c - a - 2b)$<br />C) $(a - 2b)(c + a + 2b)$<br />D) $(a + 2b)(c - a + 2b)$</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Group the terms strategically:</strong><br />
$$ac - 2bc - (a^2 - 4b^2)$$</li>
<li><strong>Factor out $c$ from the first pair and apply difference of two squares to $(a^2 - 4b^2)$:</strong><br />
$$c(a - 2b) - (a - 2b)(a + 2b)$$</li>
<li><strong>Factor out the common term $(a - 2b)$:</strong><br />
$$(a - 2b)[c - (a + 2b)] = (a - 2b)(c - a - 2b)$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B ($(a - 2b)(c - a - 2b)$)</strong></p>

<h3>2.2 Simultaneous & Quadratic Equations</h3>

<p><strong>Question 9:</strong> If the quadratic equation $x^2 - kx + 9 = 0$ has equal roots, find the possible values of $k$.</p>
<p>A) $\pm 3$<br />B) $\pm 6$<br />C) $\pm 9$<br />D) $\pm 12$</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Recall the condition for equal (repeated) roots in a quadratic equation:</strong><br />
The discriminant $D = b^2 - 4ac = 0$.</li>
<li><strong>Identify coefficients from $x^2 - kx + 9 = 0$:</strong><br />
$a = 1, b = -k, c = 9$.</li>
<li><strong>Substitute into discriminant formula and solve for $k$:</strong><br />
$$(-k)^2 - 4(1)(9) = 0 \implies k^2 - 36 = 0 \implies k^2 = 36 \implies k = \pm 6$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B ($\pm 6$)</strong></p>

<h3>2.3 Matrices and Determinants</h3>

<p><strong>Question 10:</strong> Find the determinant of matrix $P = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 1 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$.</p>
<p>A) 5<br />B) 8<br />C) 11<br />D) 14</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Apply the 2x2 determinant formula $\det(P) = (ad - bc)$:</strong><br />
$$\det(P) = (2 \times 4) - (3 \times 1) = 8 - 3 = 5$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: A (5)</strong></p>

<p><strong>Question 11:</strong> Given matrix $S = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}$, find matrix $T$ such that $ST = I$, where $I$ is the identity matrix.</p>
<p>A) $\begin{pmatrix} -2 & -1 \\ -1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$<br />B) $\begin{pmatrix} -2 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$<br />C) $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$<br />D) $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Recognize that $ST = I$ means $T$ is the inverse matrix $S^{-1}$.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Formula for inverse of matrix $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$ is $\frac{1}{\det(S)} \begin{pmatrix} d & -b \\ -c & a \end{pmatrix}$.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Calculate $\det(S)$:</strong><br />
$$\det(S) = (-1)(-2) - (1)(1) = 2 - 1 = 1$$</li>
<li><strong>Construct $S^{-1}$:</strong><br />
$$S^{-1} = \frac{1}{1} \begin{pmatrix} -2 & -1 \\ -1 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & -1 \\ -1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: A ($\begin{pmatrix} -2 & -1 \\ -1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$)</strong></p>

<h3>2.4 Binary Operations</h3>

<p><strong>Question 12:</strong> A binary operation $\circ$ on real numbers is defined by $a \circ b = a + b + 2ab$. Find the identity element $e$.</p>
<p>A) 0<br />B) 1<br />C) -1<br />D) $\frac{1}{2}$</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Recall the identity element property:</strong><br />
$$a \circ e = a$$</li>
<li><strong>Substitute $e$ into the operation definition:</strong><br />
$$a + e + 2ae = a$$</li>
<li><strong>Subtract $a$ from both sides:</strong><br />
$$e + 2ae = 0$$</li>
<li><strong>Factor out $e$:</strong><br />
$$e(1 + 2a) = 0 \implies e = 0$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: A (0)</strong></p>
</section>

<section id="section-3-geometry-trigonometry">
<h2>Section 3: Geometry & Trigonometry Questions & Solutions</h2>

<p>This section covers angles of elevation/depression, Euclidean plane geometry, circle theorems, coordinate geometry, and mensuration of 2D and 3D geometric solids.</p>

<h3>3.1 Euclidean Geometry & Polygons</h3>

<p><strong>Question 13:</strong> The interior angles of a regular polygon each measure $140^\circ$. How many sides does the polygon have?</p>
<p>A) 8<br />B) 9<br />C) 10<br />D) 12</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Calculate the exterior angle:</strong><br />
$$\text{Exterior Angle} = 180^\circ - \text{Interior Angle} = 180^\circ - 140^\circ = 40^\circ$$</li>
<li><strong>Use the total sum of exterior angles ($360^\circ$) to find number of sides ($n$):</strong><br />
$$n = \frac{360^\circ}{\text{Exterior Angle}} = \frac{360^\circ}{40^\circ} = 9$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B (9 sides - Nonagon)</strong></p>

<h3>3.2 Circle Theorems & Mensuration</h3>

<p><strong>Question 14:</strong> Find the area of a sector of a circle of radius $7\text{ cm}$ which subtends an angle of $60^\circ$ at the center. (Take $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$).</p>
<p>A) $25.67\text{ cm}^2$<br />B) $20.12\text{ cm}^2$<br />C) $25.67\text{ cm}^2$<br />D) $25.67\text{ cm}^2$</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Write the formula for area of a sector:</strong><br />
$$\text{Area} = \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} \times \pi r^2$$</li>
<li><strong>Substitute $\theta = 60^\circ$, $r = 7$, $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$:</strong><br />
$$\text{Area} = \frac{60}{360} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 7^2 = \frac{1}{6} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 49$$</li>
<li><strong>Cancel common terms:</strong><br />
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{6} \times 22 \times 7 = \frac{154}{6} = 25.67\text{ cm}^2$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: A ($25.67\text{ cm}^2$)</strong></p>

<h3>3.3 Coordinate Geometry</h3>

<p><strong>Question 15:</strong> Find the gradient of the straight line passing through points $A(2, -3)$ and $B(6, 5)$.</p>
<p>A) 1<br />B) 2<br />C) -2<br />D) 4</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Apply slope formula $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$:</strong><br />
$$m = \frac{5 - (-3)}{6 - 2} = \frac{5 + 3}{4} = \frac{8}{4} = 2$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B (2)</strong></p>

<h3>3.4 Trigonometry</h3>

<p><strong>Question 16:</strong> If $\sin \theta = \frac{3}{5}$ where $\theta$ is an acute angle, find $\tan \theta$.</p>
<p>A) $\frac{3}{4}$<br />B) $\frac{4}{3}$<br />C) $\frac{4}{5}$<br />D) $\frac{5}{3}$</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Represent the right-angled triangle ratio:</strong><br />
$$\sin \theta = \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Hypotenuse}} = \frac{3}{5}$$</li>
<li><strong>Use Pythagoras theorem to find the adjacent side:</strong><br />
$$\text{Adjacent} = \sqrt{\text{Hypotenuse}^2 - \text{Opposite}^2} = \sqrt{5^2 - 3^2} = \sqrt{25 - 9} = \sqrt{16} = 4$$</li>
<li><strong>Calculate $\tan \theta$:</strong><br />
$$\tan \theta = \frac{\text{Opposite}}{\text{Adjacent}} = \frac{3}{4}$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: A ($\frac{3}{4}$)</strong></p>
</section>

<section id="section-4-calculus">
<h2>Section 4: Calculus Past Questions & Solutions</h2>

<p>UTME Calculus tests fundamental rules of differentiation (Power, Product, Quotient, Chain rules), turning points/stationary points, and definite/indefinite integration.</p>

<h3>4.1 Differentiation</h3>

<p><strong>Question 17:</strong> Find the derivative of $y = 3x^4 - 5x^2 + 2x - 7$ with respect to $x$.</p>
<p>A) $12x^3 - 10x + 2$<br />B) $12x^3 - 5x + 2$<br />C) $6x^3 - 10x^2 + 2$<br />D) $12x^3 - 10x$</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Apply power rule $\frac{d}{dx}(ax^n) = n \cdot a x^{n-1}$ term-by-term:</strong><br />
$$\frac{d}{dx}(3x^4) = 4 \times 3x^{4-1} = 12x^3$$<br />
$$\frac{d}{dx}(-5x^2) = 2 \times (-5)x^{2-1} = -10x$$<br />
$$\frac{d}{dx}(2x) = 2$$<br />
$$\frac{d}{dx}(-7) = 0$$</li>
<li><strong>Combine terms:</strong><br />
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = 12x^3 - 10x + 2$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: A ($12x^3 - 10x + 2$)</strong></p>

<p><strong>Question 18:</strong> Find the gradient of the curve $y = 2x^3 - x^2 + 4$ at the point where $x = 2$.</p>
<p>A) 20<br />B) 22<br />C) 24<br />D) 28</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Differentiate $y$ to get gradient function $\frac{dy}{dx}$:</strong><br />
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = 6x^2 - 2x$$</li>
<li><strong>Substitute $x = 2$:</strong><br />
$$\text{Gradient} = 6(2)^2 - 2(2) = 6(4) - 4 = 24 - 4 = 20$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: A (20)</strong></p>

<h3>4.2 Integration</h3>

<p><strong>Question 19:</strong> Evaluate the definite integral $\int_{1}^{3} (3x^2 + 2x) \, dx$.</p>
<p>A) 32<br />B) 34<br />C) 36<br />D) 38</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Integrate the polynomial function term-by-term:</strong><br />
$$\int (3x^2 + 2x) \, dx = \left[ \frac{3x^3}{3} + \frac{2x^2}{2} \right] = \left[ x^3 + x^2 \right]$$</li>
<li><strong>Apply upper boundary ($x = 3$) and lower boundary ($x = 1$):</strong><br />
$$\text{Upper Limit Value} = (3)^3 + (3)^2 = 27 + 9 = 36$$<br />
$$\text{Lower Limit Value} = (1)^3 + (1)^2 = 1 + 1 = 2$$</li>
<li><strong>Subtract lower limit from upper limit:</strong><br />
$$\text{Result} = 36 - 2 = 34$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B (34)</strong></p>
</section>

<section id="section-5-statistics-probability">
<h2>Section 5: Statistics & Probability Questions & Solutions</h2>

<p>Statistics and Probability questions examine measures of location, dispersion, counting principles, and standard probability axioms.</p>

<h3>5.1 Statistics & Dispersion</h3>

<p><strong>Question 20:</strong> Calculate the mean of the numbers: 12, 15, 18, 21, 24.</p>
<p>A) 16<br />B) 18<br />C) 20<br />D) 21</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Sum all given numbers:</strong><br />
$$\sum x = 12 + 15 + 18 + 21 + 24 = 90$$</li>
<li><strong>Divide by total count ($N = 5$):</strong><br />
$$\text{Mean } \bar{x} = \frac{90}{5} = 18$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B (18)</strong></p>

<h3>5.2 Permutations & Combinations</h3>

<p><strong>Question 21:</strong> In how many ways can a committee of 3 people be selected from 7 candidates?</p>
<p>A) 21<br />B) 35<br />C) 42<br />D) 210</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Recognize this as a Combination problem (order does not matter):</strong><br />
$$^n C_r = \frac{n!}{(n-r)! r!}$$</li>
<li><strong>Substitute $n = 7$ and $r = 3$:</strong><br />
$$^7 C_3 = \frac{7!}{4! \times 3!} = \frac{7 \times 6 \times 5 \times 4!}{4! \times (3 \times 2 \times 1)} = \frac{7 \times 6 \times 5}{6} = 35$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: B (35)</strong></p>

<h3>5.3 Probability</h3>

<p><strong>Question 22:</strong> A fair die is rolled twice. What is the probability of obtaining a total sum of 8?</p>
<p>A) $\frac{5}{36}$<br />B) $\frac{1}{6}$<br />C) $\frac{7}{36}$<br />D) $\frac{1}{4}$</p>

<p><strong>Step-by-Step Solution:</strong></p>
<ol class="steps">
<li><strong>Determine total sample space size for two 6-sided dice:</strong><br />
$$N(S) = 6 \times 6 = 36$$</li>
<li><strong>List all outcomes yielding a sum of 8:</strong><br />
$$(2,6), (3,5), (4,4), (5,3), (6,2) \implies 5 \text{ outcomes}$$</li>
<li><strong>Calculate probability:</strong><br />
$$P(\text{Sum} = 8) = \frac{5}{36}$$</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Correct Answer: A ($\frac{5}{36}$)</strong></p>
</section>
