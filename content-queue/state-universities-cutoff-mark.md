<h3>Featured Snippet: State University Cut-off Mark Summary (2026/2027)</h3>
<p>State university cut-off marks for 2026 range from <strong>140 to 195</strong> depending on the institution and course of study.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Lowest General Baseline Cut-off Mark:</strong> 140 (Accepted by 18 state universities)</li>
<li><strong>Highest General Baseline Cut-off Mark:</strong> 195 (Lagos State University &amp; LASUSTECH)</li>

<div class="callout disclaimer">
<h3>Policy Revision &amp; General Disclaimer</h3>
<p>University senate councils retain full legal authority to revise entry baselines, screening formats, and departmental cut-off mark requirements without prior notice. Figures in this guide represent officially published institutional baselines alongside historical admission trends. Where an institution has not published its 2026 bulletin, figures are clearly marked as <em>Previous session baseline (awaiting official confirmation)</em>. Candidates must confirm final screening dates on official university domains ending in <code>.edu.ng</code>.</p>
</div>

<section>
<p>Securing admission into a Nigerian tertiary institution requires understanding how entry thresholds function across different schools. If you sat for the Unified Tertiary Matriculation Examination recently, finding the official <strong>state universities cut off mark</strong> for your chosen course is your immediate priority. Every academic session, the Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board determines a national baseline during its policy meeting in Abuja. However, individual state institutions retain autonomy to set their institutional baseline cut-off mark and departmental screening benchmarks based on candidate volume and classroom carrying capacity.</p>

<p>Navigating this admission cycle demands verified data rather than online speculation. A candidate scoring 180 might gain entry into an Agricultural Economics program at a state school in the North, yet face immediate rejection for Computer Science at a southwestern state institution with that same score. Understanding how these thresholds operate, how aggregate scores are computed, and what alternatives exist if you miss the target score can make the difference between securing a university seat this year or sitting out another entrance exam.</p>
</section>

<section id="glossary-of-key-nigerian-tertiary-admission-terms">
<h2>Glossary of Key Nigerian Tertiary Admission Terms</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Institutional Baseline Cut-off Mark:</strong> The minimum UTME score set by a university senate required to purchase a Post-UTME screening form or register for online credential verification.</li>
<li><strong>Departmental Aggregate Cut-off Mark:</strong> The final combined score needed to gain admission into a specific course after screening scores, UTME marks, and O'Level grades are evaluated.</li>
<li><strong>JAMB Policy Benchmark:</strong> The minimum score agreed upon during the annual national policy meeting in Abuja below which no degree-awarding institution may admit students. Check the general <a href="/jamb-cut-off-mark">JAMB Cut Off Mark Guide</a> for overall national rules.</li>
<li><strong>Historical Admission Trend:</strong> Past score averages compiled over recent admission cycles (2023–2025) used to estimate candidate competitiveness.</li>
<li><strong>JAMB CAPS:</strong> Joint Admissions and Matriculation Board Central Admissions Processing System. Review our complete <a href="/jamb-caps-guide">JAMB CAPS Guide</a> to monitor admission offers.</li>
<li><strong>JAMB IBASS:</strong> Integrated Brochure and Syllabus System. The official repository for entry requirements, subject combinations, and course waivers. Consult the <a href="/jamb-subject-combination">JAMB Subject Combination Guide</a> for details.</li>
<li><strong>Catchment Area Policy:</strong> A geographic policy where candidates from states neighboring or hosting a state university receive specific quota considerations. Read the <a href="/catchment-area-guide">Catchment Area Guide</a> for details.</li>
<li><strong>Educationally Less Developed States (ELDS):</strong> Special policy considerations applied in tertiary admissions. Learn more in our <a href="/elds-guide">ELDS Guide</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Indigene Quota:</strong> A state preference policy allocating a percentage of available admission slots to residents or originators of the host state.</li>
</ul>
</section>

<h2>Research Methodology &amp; Fact-Checking Policy</h2>
<p>To maintain factual confidence and prevent candidate misinformation, data in this document was compiled using a four-step auditing framework:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Official Policy Audit:</strong> Baseline parameters were verified against decisions reached during national admission policy meetings chaired by JAMB leadership.</li>
<li><strong>Registrar Notice Audit:</strong> Institutional baseline figures were cross-referenced against official screening notices published by university registrars on verified <code>.edu.ng</code> domains. Unverified third-party blogs were excluded.</li>
<li><strong>Regulatory Body Audits:</strong> Course capacity limits were verified against professional quota releases from the <a href="https://www.nuc.edu.ng" target="_blank" rel="noopener">National Universities Commission (NUC)</a>, Medical and Dental Council of Nigeria (MDCN), Council of Legal Education (CLE), and <a href="https://www.nmcn.gov.ng" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria (NMCN)</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Clear Score Disaggregation:</strong> Where an institution's senate has not published its 2026 screening bulletin at the time of review, its status is explicitly marked as <em>Previous session baseline (awaiting official confirmation)</em>. All departmental course-level targets are explicitly labeled as <em>Historical Admission Trends</em>.</li>
</ol>

<section id="how-university-admissions-work-in-nigeria">
<h2>How University Admissions Work in Nigeria</h2>
<p>Many applicants mistake getting the institutional baseline cut-off mark for a guarantee of admission. In reality, tertiary admissions operate across four distinct quota pillars:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Merit Quota:</strong> Granted strictly to the highest aggregate scorers regardless of state of origin.</li>
<li><strong>State Indigene / Catchment Quota:</strong> In state universities, this policy reserves a designated percentage of available seats for candidates originating from the host state or specified catchment LGAs.</li>
<li><strong>Educationally Disadvantaged Quotas:</strong> Some state institutions apply lower entry baselines to candidates from rural or educationally less developed local government areas within the state.</li>
<li><strong>Discretionary / Senate Quota:</strong> Special admissions approved by university senate leadership for staff development, sports excellence, or institutional partnerships.</li>
</ul>

<div class="callout">
<p><strong>Note on Quota Misconceptions:</strong> Federal university quota ratios do not apply uniformly to state universities. Compare these rules with our <a href="/federal-universities-cut-off-marks">Federal Universities Cut Off Marks Guide</a> and <a href="/private-universities-cut-off-marks">Private Universities Cut Off Marks Guide</a>.</p>
<p><strong>Federal vs. State Institution Note:</strong> Institutions like Joseph Sarwuan Tarka University Makurdi (formerly FUAM) are federal universities governed by federal guidelines, which is why they are excluded from this state university directory.</p>
</div>

<h3>Why Cut-off Marks Change Annually</h3>
<p>State universities adjust cut-off mark thresholds each session due to three main factors:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Annual UTME Score Distribution:</strong> If national performance in core subjects drops, university senates lower entry floors to fill departments.</li>
<li><strong>Application Volume Spikes:</strong> When candidate applications for courses like Software Engineering or Nursing double, departments raise aggregate cut-off mark requirements to manage applicant volume.</li>
<li><strong>Professional Body Accreditation Caps:</strong> If the MDCN or Nursing Council reduces a university's intake quota from 100 to 50 students due to facility audits, the department must raise its cut-off mark significantly.</li>
</ol>
</section>

<section id="what-changed-2026">
<h2>What Changed in the 2026/2027 Admission Cycle?</h2>
<p>Several key adjustments took effect across state university admissions during this cycle:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Age Limitation Directives:</strong> LASU strictly enforced its requirement that candidates must turn 16 years of age on or before October 1 of the admission year.</li>
<li><strong>Increased Online Point Screenings:</strong> Institutions like UNIOSUN, EKSU, and AAUA maintained paperless O'Level point computation models, avoiding physical CBT exams to accelerate processing on JAMB CAPS.</li>
<li><strong>Expanded Allied Health Faculties:</strong> State universities like KWASU, UNIMED, and BMU expanded intakes for Nursing Science and Medical Laboratory Science following upgraded council accreditation quotas.</li>
<li><strong>Institutional Name Rebranding:</strong> Full adoption of updated branding for Sa'adu Zungur University (SAZU, formerly BASUG), Aliko Dangote University of Science and Technology (ADUSTECH, formerly KUST Wudil), and Prince Abubakar Audu University (PAAU, formerly Kogi State University).</li>
</ul>
</section>

<section id="aggregate-calculator-guide">
<h2>Step-by-Step Aggregate Score Calculator Guide</h2>

<div class="callout disclaimer">
<p><strong>Calculation Disclaimer:</strong> Aggregate score formulas differ across institutions. The formulas illustrated below represent common models used by selected state institutions (Point System vs. CBT Post-UTME) and serve as illustrative examples rather than universal standards.</p>
</div>

<h3>Method 1: Point-System Screening Model (LASU / OOU Format)</h3>
<p>This model evaluates your UTME score alongside your five relevant O'Level subject grades. Physical entrance examinations are omitted.</p>

<h4>Standard Point Scale:</h4>
<p>A1 = 8 Points | B2 = 7 Points | B3 = 6 Points | C4 = 5 Points | C5 = 4 Points | C6 = 3 Points</p>

<h4>Mathematical Formula:</h4>
<p><code>UTME Weight = (Candidate UTME Score / 400) × 50</code></p>
<p><code>O'Level Weight = (Total Grade Points in 5 Subjects / 40) × 50</code></p>
<p><code>Final Aggregate Score = UTME Weight + O'Level Weight</code></p>

<h4>Practical Worked Example:</h4>
<p>Suppose candidate <strong>Amina</strong> applies for Computer Science with a UTME score of <strong>240</strong> and holds these five relevant WAEC grades: Math (A1), English (B2), Physics (B2), Chemistry (B3), Further Math (B3).</p>

<ol>
<li><strong>UTME Component:</strong><br>UTME Weight = (240 / 400) × 50 = 0.60 × 50 = <strong>30.00</strong></li>
<li><strong>O'Level Component:</strong><br>Grade Points = 8 (A1) + 7 (B2) + 7 (B2) + 6 (B3) + 6 (B3) = <strong>34 points</strong><br>O'Level Weight = (34 / 40) × 50 = 0.85 × 50 = <strong>42.50</strong></li>
<li><strong>Total Aggregate Score:</strong><br>Final Aggregate Score = 30.00 + 42.50 = <strong>72.50%</strong></li>
</ol>
<p>If the department's final aggregate benchmark is set at <strong>68%</strong>, Amina clears the threshold.</p>

<hr>

<h3>Method 2: CBT Examination Model (DELSU / RSU Format)</h3>
<p>In this format, candidates take a Computer-Based Test conducted directly by the university.</p>

<h4>Standard 50/50 Formula:</h4>
<p><code>UTME Weight = Candidate UTME Score / 8</code></p>
<p><code>Post-UTME Weight = Post-UTME Exam Score (out of 100) / 2</code></p>
<p><code>Final Aggregate Score = UTME Weight + Post-UTME Weight</code></p>

<h4>Practical Worked Example:</h4>
<p>Suppose candidate <strong>Emeka</strong> scores <strong>220</strong> in UTME and scores <strong>60/100</strong> in the university's Post-UTME examination.</p>

<ol>
<li><strong>UTME Component:</strong><br>UTME Weight = 220 / 8 = <strong>27.50</strong></li>
<li><strong>Post-UTME Component:</strong><br>Post-UTME Weight = 60 / 2 = <strong>30.00</strong></li>
<li><strong>Total Aggregate Score:</strong><br>Final Aggregate Score = 27.50 + 30.00 = <strong>57.50%</strong></li>
</ol>
<p>If the mechanical engineering departmental cut-off mark is <strong>55%</strong>, Emeka satisfies the admission standard.</p>

<div class="callout">
<h4>💡 Callout: Admission Myths Busted</h4>
<ul>
<li><strong>Myth 1: "Scoring 200 in UTME guarantees admission into Medicine at a State University."</strong><br>
<em>Reality:</em> False. Medical faculties operate under strict MDCN quotas. Historical aggregate trends show medical entry typically requires 250+ in UTME combined with top O'Level grades.</li>
<li><strong>Myth 2: "State Universities only admit indigenes."</strong><br>
<em>Reality:</em> False. State universities reserve admission slots for non-indigenes on open merit, though non-indigenes often face slightly higher aggregate score thresholds.</li>
<li><strong>Myth 3: "You can combine WAEC and NABTEB results for general university degrees."</strong><br>
<em>Reality:</em> False. Most state university senates do not allow combining WAEC with NABTEB for general academic programs.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<section id="faq">
<h2>Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)</h2>

<h3>Which state university accepts 140 cut-off mark?</h3>
<p>Institutions like Abia State University (ABSU), Ebonyi State University (EBSU), Ambrose Alli University (AAU Ekpoma), Prince Abubakar Audu University (PAAU), Sule Lamido University (SLU), UNICROSS Calabar, and Sokoto State University (SSU) maintain an institutional baseline cut-off mark of 140 for many general degree programs.</p>

<h3>Which state university accepts 150 cut-off mark?</h3>
<p>State universities accepting 150 include Chukwuemeka Odumegwu Ojukwu University (COOU), Imo State University (IMSU), Benue State University (BSU), Ibrahim Badamasi Babangida University (IBBU), and Gombe State University (GSU) for non-health science tracks.</p>

<h3>Can I study Nursing with a 180 UTME score at a state university?</h3>
<p>Scoring 180 meets the screening baseline at UNIOSUN, DELSU, or ABSU. However, historical aggregate trends indicate that final admission into Nursing Science typically requires aggregate scores corresponding to a UTME performance of 220–240+ due to intense candidate competition and NMCN intake caps.</p>

<h3>Which state university has the lowest cut-off mark in Nigeria?</h3>
<p>State universities in Northern and Eastern zones—such as ABSU, EBSU, TSU Jalingo, ADSU Mubi, and SLU Kafin Hausa—maintain the lowest general entry floors at the national baseline of 140.</p>

<h3>Which state universities accept second choice candidates?</h3>
<p>While most southwestern state schools require first-choice status, institutions like Olabisi Onabanjo University (OOU), Kwara State University (KWASU), Abia State University (ABSU), and Nasarawa State University (NSUK) allow candidates to switch to them as first choice after results drop.</p>

<h3>Which state university does not write Post-UTME exams?</h3>
<p>Institutions such as Lagos State University (LASU), Osun State University (UNIOSUN), Adekunle Ajasin University (AAUA), Ekiti State University (EKSU), and Enugu State University of Science and Technology (ESUT) operate paperless online O'Level point screening without writing physical CBT entrance exams.</p>

<h3>Which state university accepts awaiting result candidates?</h3>
<p>Most state universities allow candidates to register for screening with awaiting results. However, official O'Level grades must be uploaded to JAMB CAPS prior to the release of primary merit admission lists.</p>

<h3>Which state university has the cheapest school fees?</h3>
<p>State institutions in northern regions (such as UMYU Katsina, SSU Sokoto, and KSUSTA Aliero) generally maintain lower tuition fee structures compared to state universities in southern regions.</p>

<h3>Can I gain admission if I score exactly the institutional cut-off mark?</h3>
<p>Scoring exactly the institutional baseline cut-off mark makes you eligible for screening. You can secure admission into less competitive or undersubscribed courses if your O'Level grades or screening performance keep your aggregate score competitive.</p>
</section>

<div class="callout summary key-takeaways">
<h2>Key Takeaways</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>General Baseline vs. Departmental Cut-off Mark:</strong> Meeting a university's general baseline score (e.g., 160 or 195) qualifies you for screening, but final admission depends on your departmental aggregate score.</li>
<li><strong>Dynamic Thresholds:</strong> Cut-off mark figures shift every session based on applicant performance, regulatory quotas (MDCN, CLE, NMCN), and state guidelines.</li>
<li><strong>Calculation Models Vary:</strong> Aggregate formulas differ. Point-system institutions weight O'Level grades heavily, while CBT institutions weight screening exam scores heavily.</li>
<li><strong>Verify Official Sources:</strong> Consult official <code>.edu.ng</code> web domains, signed registry releases, and the official <a href="https://www.jamb.gov.ng" target="_blank" rel="noopener">JAMB Portal</a> for accurate announcements.</li>
<li><strong>Alternative Routes:</strong> If your score falls short of target cut-off mark baselines, options like JAMB Change of Course, supplementary advert lists, JUPEB, IJMB, or university diplomas offer viable pathways to a degree.</li>
</ul>
</div>
