<section id="1-understanding-ui-cut-off-marks-the-basics">
<h2>1. Understanding UI Cut Off Marks: The Basics</h2>
<p>Admissions at the University of Ibadan are governed by two distinct grading thresholds. Understanding how they interact is essential to navigating the process.</p>

<h3>The UTME General Cut Off Mark</h3>
<p>According to the University of Ibadan Undergraduate Admissions Portal, candidates must hit a baseline score to even be considered for registration. This general minimum score has consistently been set at <strong>200</strong>. Candidates who score 199 or lower are ineligible to apply, regardless of their O'Level grades or choice of course.</p>

<h3>Departmental Aggregate Cut Off Marks</h3>
<p>Meeting the general UTME score of 200 does not guarantee admission. Your final placement is determined by your Departmental Aggregate Score. This score is a combination of your JAMB score and your Post-UTME performance, calculated on a 50:50 weighted scale.</p>

<h3>How UI Calculates the Weighted Aggregate Score</h3>
<p>UI scales both examinations to a maximum of 50 points each, creating a total aggregate score out of 100. For a deep understanding of this process, candidates should refer to our detailed tutorial on <strong>how to calculate UI aggregate</strong>.</p>
<p>The calculation uses this formula:</p>
<p class="math-formula" style="text-align: center;">
Aggregate Score = (JAMB UTME Score / 8) + (Post-UTME Score / 2)
</p>
<p>Your secondary school O'Level grades do not factor into this numerical aggregate. Instead, O'Level results serve as a mandatory qualification gate. You must possess at least five credits in relevant subjects, obtained in either one or two sittings depending on the faculty.</p>

<h3>The Screening Format and Location</h3>
<p>The screening is a Computer-Based Test (CBT). Based on recent admission exercises, the test is typically held at the UI Distance Learning Centre (DLC) CBT Centre, located along the Ajibode-Sasa Road in Ibadan. The examination historically consists of 100 multiple-choice questions covering your four UTME subject combinations. Recent screening exercises have generally allotted approximately 90 minutes for candidates to complete the test.</p>
</section>

<section id="5-why-ui-departmental-cut-off-marks-fluctuate-annually">
<h2>5. Why UI Departmental Cut-Off Marks Fluctuate Annually</h2>
<p>Knowing the raw cut-offs is only half the battle. Successful applicants understand the underlying factors that shift these numbers up or down each year.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Overall Performance Density in Post-UTME:</strong> If the CBT questions align well with student preparation and average screening scores soar, the university naturally raises the departmental boundaries to avoid over-admission.</li>
<li><strong>Professional Regulatory Quotas:</strong> Bodies such as the MDCN or the Pharmacists Council of Nigeria (PCN) enforce hard capacity limits on UI. If a body declares that only 100 students can be indexed, the cut-off mark will be pushed as high as necessary to filter down to the top 100 candidates.</li>
<li><strong>JAMB Performance Inflation:</strong> When national UTME scores skew exceptionally high, the initial entry pool is stronger, elevating the baseline academic strength of applicants entering the screening stage.</li>
<li><strong>Catchment and ELDS Distribution Policies:</strong> Under federal guidelines, UI must balance merit placements (45%) with catchment states (35%) and ELDS locations (20%). The merit cutoff is always the highest, while ELDS limits drop lower depending on how candidates from those specific states perform.</li>
</ul>
</section>

<section id="7-practical-aggregate-score-calculations">
<h2>7. Practical Aggregate Score Calculations</h2>
<p>These four realistic scenarios show how the 50:50 aggregate formula operates in practice, illustrating how different score combinations play out.</p>

<h3>Example 1: High UTME Score with Average Post-UTME Performance</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>JAMB UTME Score:</strong> 312</li>
<li><strong>Post-UTME Score:</strong> 54</li>
<li><strong>Calculation:</strong> (312 / 8) + (54 / 2) = 39.0 + 27.0 = 66.0</li>
<li><strong>Result:</strong> This candidate misses the cutoff for Law or Pharmacy but comfortably qualifies for Computer Science, Civil Engineering, or Economics.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Example 2: Moderate UTME Score with High Post-UTME Performance</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>JAMB UTME Score:</strong> 248</li>
<li><strong>Post-UTME Score:</strong> 84</li>
<li><strong>Calculation:</strong> (248 / 8) + (84 / 2) = 31.0 + 42.0 = 73.0</li>
<li><strong>Result:</strong> This candidate comfortably qualifies for Law, Pharmacy, and Nursing despite a lower JAMB score.</li>
</ul>
<p><em>Expert Insight:</em> A candidate with a 248 UTME score who performs exceptionally in the Post-UTME may outperform another candidate with a 320 UTME score but a weak screening performance. Never assume a modest UTME score excludes you from highly competitive courses.</p>

<h3>Example 3: High UTME Score with Disqualifying Post-UTME Performance</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>JAMB UTME Score:</strong> 340</li>
<li><strong>Post-UTME Score:</strong> 46</li>
<li><strong>Calculation:</strong> (340 / 8) + (46 / 2) = 42.5 + 23.0 = 65.5</li>
<li><strong>Result:</strong> This candidate would generally not remain competitive for admission based on recent admission exercises. In previous cycles, candidates scoring below 50 out of 100 in the Post-UTME have been disqualified, regardless of how high their JAMB UTME score was.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Example 4: Borderline Score Scenario</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>JAMB UTME Score:</strong> 224</li>
<li><strong>Post-UTME Score:</strong> 58</li>
<li><strong>Calculation:</strong> (224 / 8) + (58 / 2) = 28.0 + 29.0 = 57.0</li>
<li><strong>Result:</strong> This candidate qualifies for many BA and BSc courses in the Faculties of Arts, Science, and Agriculture.</li>
</ul>
</section>
<section id="8-common-pitfalls-that-derail-ui-admissions">
<h2>8. Common Pitfalls That Derail UI Admissions</h2>
<p>Even the most brilliant candidates can miss out on admission due to administrative mistakes. Avoid these common errors to protect your hard work:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Failing to Upload O’Level Results on JAMB CAPS:</strong> UI cannot process admission for any candidate whose O'Level results are missing from the central JAMB portal. Upload these early.</li>
<li><strong>Incorrect UTME Subject Combinations:</strong> If you sit for subjects in JAMB that do not align with UI's specific departmental requirements (for instance, omitting Literature-in-English for Law), your application is disqualified. Check the **JAMB Subject Combination** list before registering.</li>
<li><strong>Choosing UI as a Second Choice:</strong> The university does not recognize second-choice candidates. If you did not choose UI as your first preference, use the JAMB portal to perform a **Change of Institution** before registration closes.</li>
<li><strong>Targeting the Exact Cut-Off Score:</strong> Aiming precisely for the previous year's aggregate leaves zero room for error. Always build a safety buffer of at least 3 to 5 aggregate points in your study targets.</li>
</ul>
</section>

In recent admission cycles, the registration has required a portal access fee of <strong>₦3,000</strong> alongside a screening fee of <strong>₦2,000</strong>, totaling <strong>₦5,000</strong>. Complete payment steps can be found in our <strong>UI Post-UTME Registration Guide</strong>.</p>

<h3>Navigating Departmental Cut-Off Misses (Change of Course)</h3>
<p>If you score at least 50% in the Post-UTME but do not meet your department's cut-off mark, you may still have options. In some admission cycles, the university opens a change-of-course window. Eligible candidates can log in to their portals and apply for departments with vacant spots, provided they meet the aggregate requirements for those new courses.</p>

<section id="10-frequently-asked-questions-faqs">
<h2>10. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)</h2>

<h3>Does UI conduct Post-UTME every year?</h3>
<p>Yes. Except under extraordinary circumstances or national directives, the University of Ibadan conducts a computer-based screening test annually to filter its large pool of applicants.</p>

<h3>Does UI accept a second choice of institution?</h3>
<p>No. The University of Ibadan does not offer admission to candidates who chose them as a second-choice option in JAMB. To be eligible for screening, you must set UI as your first-choice institution.</p>

<h3>Does UI accept awaiting results for Post-UTME registration?</h3>
<p>Yes. You can register for the UI Post-UTME using awaiting results. However, your completed WAEC, NECO, or NABTEB results must be uploaded to the JAMB Central Admissions Processing System (CAPS) portal before the final admission list is compiled. See our <strong>JAMB CAPS Admission Guide</strong> for step-by-step assistance.</p>

<h3>Can I change course after admission?</h3>
<p>Changing courses after you have been admitted is extremely difficult. It usually requires meeting specific academic performance criteria (typically a very high CGPA) at the end of your 100-level session and is subject to vacancy availability in the target department.</p>

<h3>How do I calculate my aggregate?</h3>
<p>Multiply your JAMB UTME score by 0.125 (or divide by 8) and your Post-UTME score by 0.5 (or divide by 2), then add the two values together.</p>

<h3>How many questions are in the UI Post-UTME?</h3>
<p>The test historically consists of 100 multiple-choice questions split equally (25 questions each) across the four subjects you sat for in your JAMB UTME.</p>

<h3>What happens if I miss the screening exam?</h3>
<p>Missing your scheduled Post-UTME screening session usually results in automatic disqualification. The university rarely schedules make-up tests unless there is a documented institutional error or a widespread technical failure.</p>

<h3>Can I combine WAEC and NECO results to gain admission?</h3>
<p>You can combine two O'Level results for courses in the Faculties of Arts, Science, Technology, and Social Sciences. However, the College of Medicine (including Medicine, Dentistry, Nursing, and Physiotherapy) and the Faculty of Pharmacy do not accept combined sittings.</p>

<h3>Does UI accept Direct Entry (DE) or JUPEB candidates?</h3>
<p>Yes. Direct Entry candidates can apply with their Advanced Level (A'Level) results, NCE, National Diploma (ND), or Higher National Diploma (HND) certificates. They must register on the UI admission portal during the designated registration window.</p>

<h3>Can I upload my O'Level results after registering for the Post-UTME?</h3>
<p>Yes. If your O'Level results are pending during the registration window, you can submit your application. However, you must upload the verified results to your JAMB portal via an approved CBT center before the university begins the final selection process.</p>

<h3>Does UI accept NABTEB results?</h3>
<p>According to the official admission brochure, UI accepts NABTEB results for specific programs, primarily in the Faculty of Technology and Faculty of Science. However, candidates should verify departmental exceptions prior to submitting registration fees.</p>

<h3>Can I change my institution after registering for the Post-UTME?</h3>
<p>No. Once you register for the UI Post-UTME screening, your details are locked within the university's database. Initiating a change of institution afterwards on the JAMB portal will disrupt your profile and likely nullify your screening eligibility.</p>

<h3>How many candidates does UI admit annually?</h3>
<p>While over 20,000 candidates typically sit for the exams or apply, the university's approved undergraduate admission capacity historically ranges between 4,000 and 4,500 students per session due to NUC quota restrictions.</p>

<h3>Does UI use catchment parameters for every course?</h3>
<p>Yes, but with major limitations. While catchment parameters are applied to balance geographic representation, the extremely high number of competitive applicants from South-West Nigeria often causes the catchment cut-offs for top courses (like Medicine and Law) to be identical to the Merit cut-off scores.</p>
</section>
<section id="final-thoughts-preparation-strategy">
<h2>Final Thoughts &amp; Preparation Strategy</h2>
<p>Successfully securing admission to the University of Ibadan depends heavily on your preparation for the Post-UTME. Because the screening accounts for 50% of your final aggregate, an excellent Post-UTME score can help offset a modest JAMB result. Candidates preparing for the exam are encouraged to practise with authentic and up-to-date UI Post-UTME past questions to understand past trends and review the official <strong>UI Post-UTME Syllabus</strong> carefully.</p>
<p>Since official departmental cut-off marks are released only after the screening exercise, candidates should aim to exceed previous years' benchmarks rather than merely meeting them. Preparing early and targeting a higher aggregate significantly improves admission prospects.</p>
<p>Ensure that you register with active contact details and upload your O'Level results to JAMB CAPS as soon as they are available. Candidates are advised to monitor the official University of Ibadan Admissions Portal rather than relying solely on third-party websites to track the release of the final <strong>UI Admission List</strong>.</p>
<p>To check the financial implications of your study, you may also consult our comprehensive overview of <strong>UI School Fees</strong>, <strong>UI Acceptance Fee</strong>, and <strong>UI Hostel Accommodation</strong> guidelines once your admission is secured. To stay updated with key academic timelines, check the latest <strong>UI Academic Calendar</strong>. Bookmark this guide to keep track of these baseline targets as the 2027/2028 admission window approaches.</p>
