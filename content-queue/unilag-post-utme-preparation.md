<h2>1. Understanding the UNILAG Post UTME: Core Policies</h2>

<h3>Purpose and Importance of the Screening</h3>
<p>The University of Lagos (UNILAG) remains one of Nigeria’s most competitive higher institutions. Historical statistics show that UNILAG receives more than 60,000 applications from hopeful students across the nation during the JAMB registration period. Out of this massive crowd, the university routinely has capacity to admit only around 7,000 to 10,000 candidates across all faculties.</p>

<p>On average, UNILAG admits less than 15% of applicants across all faculties. This means your national UTME performance is only the first hurdle. The Post UTME screening is designed as an institutional mechanism to filter out academically inconsistent profiles and identify candidates capable of handling rigorous university coursework.</p>

<p>Let's look at an illustrative, hypothetical scenario to see why preparing for this stage is so critical:</p>

<blockquote>
<strong>Illustrative Scenario A (Fictional Example):</strong><br>
Aisha scored a brilliant 315 in her JAMB UTME. She felt secure. She assumed her admission to study Law was a done deal. Aisha did not prepare seriously for the Post UTME format and ran out of time on the day of the exam. She scored 11 out of 30. Because she fell below the required baseline score of 12 for that section, she was disqualified. Her high JAMB score could not save her. This scenario serves as an important case study showing why thoroughly reviewing exam materials matters.
</blockquote>

<h3>The Aggregate Score Formula</h3>
<p>According to official guidelines from recent admission cycles (such as the 2025/2026 and 2026/2027 exercises), your ultimate admission standing is determined by an aggregate score computed over 100 points.</p>

<p>The formula works as follows:</p>

<div class="math-block">
Final Aggregate = (UTME Score / 8) + O'Level Points (Max 20) + Post UTME Score (Max 30)
</div>

<h4>The O'Level Scoring System</h4>
<p>UNILAG assigns numeric points to the grades of the five compulsory subjects required for your specific department:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>A1:</strong> 4.0 points</li>
<li><strong>B2:</strong> 3.6 points</li>
<li><strong>B3:</strong> 3.2 points</li>
<li><strong>C4:</strong> 2.8 points</li>
<li><strong>C5:</strong> 2.4 points</li>
<li><strong>C6:</strong> 2.0 points</li>
<li><strong>D7, E8, F9:</strong> 0.0 points</li>
</ul>

<p>To calculate your O'Level contribution, add the point values of your five required subjects. The maximum possible total is 20 points ($5 \text{ subjects} \times 4.0 = 20.0$ points).</p>

<blockquote>
<strong>Illustrative Scenario B (Fictional Example):</strong><br>
Let's look at Chinedu, a hypothetical applicant aiming for Mechanical Engineering.
<ul>
<li><strong>Chinedu's UTME Score:</strong> 288
<ul>
<li><em>UTME contribution:</em> 288 / 8 = 36.0 points</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Chinedu's O'Level Grades</strong> (Mathematics, English Language, Physics, Chemistry, Further Mathematics):
<ul>
<li>Mathematics: A1 (4.0 points)</li>
<li>English Language: B2 (3.6 points)</li>
<li>Physics: B3 (3.2 points)</li>
<li>Chemistry: B2 (3.6 points)</li>
<li>Further Mathematics: C4 (2.8 points)</li>
<li><em>O'Level contribution:</em> 4.0 + 3.6 + 3.2 + 3.6 + 2.8 = 17.2 points</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Chinedu's Post UTME Score:</strong> 23 out of 30</li>
</ul>
Chinedu's final aggregate score is:
<div class="math-block">
Aggregate = 36.0 (UTME) + 17.2 (O'Level) + 23.0 (Post UTME) = 76.2%
</div>
</blockquote>

<p><em>Note: The university may adjust this formula depending on the admission year, so always check the latest notifications on the official portal.</em></p>

<h3>Eligibility Criteria</h3>
<p>To register for the screening, candidates must meet the following eligibility requirements:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>First-Choice Selection:</strong> You must have designated UNILAG as your first-choice institution in the UTME. If you chose another school, you must perform a <a href="/jamb-change-of-institution">JAMB Change of Institution</a> on the JAMB portal before registering.</li>
<li><strong>The 200-Score Baseline:</strong> You must score 200 or above in the UTME. If your score is between 150 and 199, you are ineligible to apply.</li>
<li><strong>One-Sitting O'Level Requirement:</strong> Candidates must have five credit passes in relevant subjects, including English Language and Mathematics, obtained in a <strong>single sitting</strong>. UNILAG does not accept combined O'Level results (like WAEC + NECO) for UTME admissions.</li>
<li><strong>Age Boundary:</strong> You must meet the age limit. For the 2026/2027 cycle, candidates who would not be 16 years of age by September 30, 2026, were ineligible and advised not to apply.</li>
</ol>

<h2>2. Deconstructing the UNILAG Post UTME Exam Structure</h2>

<h3>Standard Exam Format</h3>
<p>For the 2025/2026 and 2026/2027 exercises, the UNILAG Post UTME was an online, remotely proctored test. The exam features <strong>40 multiple-choice questions</strong> to be answered in <strong>30 minutes</strong>.</p>

<p>This is a fast-paced exam. You have an average of 45 seconds per question.</p>

<p>The typical question layout is:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>English Language:</strong> 20 Questions</li>
<li><strong>Mathematics:</strong> 10 Questions</li>
<li><strong>General Paper:</strong> 10 Questions</li>
</ul>

<p>While this has been the standard format in recent sessions, the university reserves the right to adjust question volumes or subject weighting.</p>

<h3>Subject Breakdown</h3>

<h4>English Language</h4>
<p>This section makes up half of your exam. The focus is on grammar rules and reading speed.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Grammar Mechanics:</strong> Questions target subject-verb agreement (concord), correct prepositions, active and passive voices, and structural errors.</li>
<li><strong>Lexis and Structure:</strong> You will identify synonyms, antonyms, register words, and idiomatic expressions.</li>
<li><strong>Comprehension Passages:</strong> These are short but dense. You must read quickly to find the main idea and identify tone or logical implications.</li>
</ul>

<h4>Mathematics</h4>
<p>The math section tests basic mathematical skills. Since calculators are not permitted, the questions focus on quick calculations and mental arithmetic.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Core Topics:</strong> Focus on indices, logarithms, basic algebraic equations, simple probability, permutations, combinations, and statistics (mean, median, and mode).</li>
</ul>

<h4>General Paper</h4>
<p>This section tests your knowledge of history, current affairs, and basic logical reasoning.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Topics covered:</strong> Key moments in Nigerian history, basic geography, and simple questions about UNILAG itself, such as its founding year (1962).</li>
</ul>

<h3>Faculty-Specific Exams: A Common Misconception</h3>
<p>Many candidates waste time studying specialized subjects like Physics, Government, or Chemistry.</p>

<p><strong>UNILAG does not set department-specific or faculty-specific exams for UTME candidates.</strong></p>

<p>Whether you are applying for Medicine, Law, Mass Communication, or Civil Engineering, <strong>everyone writes the exact same general test</strong> containing English, Mathematics, and General Paper. A student aiming for Music answers the same questions as a student aiming for Electrical Engineering.</p>

<h2>5. Mastering the UNILAG Post UTME Syllabus: Topic Deep Dive</h2>

<h3>English Language Deep Dive</h3>

<h4>Advanced Grammar Rules and Concord Traps</h4>
<p>Subject-verb agreement (concord) is a major focus of the English section. Pay close attention to these common grammatical traps:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Collective Nouns in Context:</strong> A collective noun takes a singular verb when the group acts as a single unit, but a plural verb when the members act individually.
<ul>
<li><em>Incorrect:</em> The jury were unanimous in its decision.</li>
<li><em>Correct:</em> The jury <strong>was</strong> unanimous in its decision. (Acting as one unit).</li>
<li><em>Incorrect:</em> The committee is divided on the issue.</li>
<li><em>Correct:</em> The committee <strong>are</strong> divided on the issue. (Acting as individuals with different opinions).</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Indefinite Pronouns:</strong> Pronouns like <em>each, everyone, someone, neither, and either</em> are grammatically singular and require singular verbs.
<ul>
<li><em>Incorrect:</em> Neither of the two files have been modified.</li>
<li><em>Correct:</em> Neither of the two files <strong>has</strong> been modified.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Phrasal Prepositions:</strong> When a subject is connected to another noun using phrases like <em>as well as, together with, along with, or accompanied by</em>, the verb must agree with the first, main subject.
<ul>
<li><em>Incorrect:</em> The lecturer along with his students are attending the seminar.</li>
<li><em>Correct:</em> The lecturer along with his students <strong>is</strong> attending the seminar.</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h4>Lexis, Structure, and Register Examples</h4>
<p>Register questions test your familiarity with terms used in specific fields. You must choose the word that fits the context of the sentence:</p>
<ul>
<li><em>Example (Medical Register):</em> "The surgeon noted that the patient's condition had begun to __________."
<ul>
<li>(A) decompose (B) deteriorate (C) decrease (D) devalue</li>
<li><strong>Answer:</strong> (B) deteriorate. In a medical context, "deteriorate" is the correct term to describe a worsening physical state.</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h4>Cloze Test Structure</h4>
<p>Cloze tests present a passage with blank spaces where words have been removed. You must select the most appropriate word for each space based on the overall context:</p>
<ul>
<li><em>Example:</em> "Modern banking relies heavily on electronic technology. To prevent unauthorized access, servers are protected by secure __________."
<ul>
<li>(A) corridors (B) firewalls (C) accounts (D) parameters</li>
<li><strong>Answer:</strong> (B) firewalls. This is the correct technical term within an information security context.</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h4>Word Stress and Phonology</h4>
<p>Word stress questions test your pronunciation rules. Focus on these core patterns:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Two-Syllable Verbs vs. Nouns:</strong> For many two-syllable words, the stress falls on the first syllable if it is a noun, but on the second syllable if it is a verb.
<ul>
<li><em>Noun:</em> <strong>EX</strong>port (e.g., "The country’s major export is petroleum.")</li>
<li><em>Verb:</em> ex<strong>PORT</strong> (e.g., "We must export agricultural goods.")</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Unstressed Syllables with Silent Letters:</strong> Identify words with silent letters to avoid pronunciation traps:
<ul>
<li>In the word <em>subtle</em>, the "b" is silent (/ˈsʌt.əl/).</li>
<li>In the word <em>comb</em>, the "b" is silent (/koʊm/).</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h3>Mathematics Mastery</h3>

<h4>Algebra and Logarithms</h4>
<div class="math-block">
log<sub>a</sub>(xy) = log<sub>a</sub>(x) + log<sub>a</sub>(y) &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;and&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; log<sub>a</sub>(x/y) = log<sub>a</sub>(x) - log<sub>a</sub>(y)
</div>
<ul>
<li><strong>Sample Question:</strong> Simplify the expression without using a calculator: log<sub>10</sub> 25 + log<sub>10</sub> 4
<ul>
<li><strong>Solution:</strong> Apply the addition rule of logarithms:
<div class="math-block">
log<sub>10</sub> 25 + log<sub>10</sub> 4 = log<sub>10</sub>(25 &times; 4) = log<sub>10</sub> 100
</div>
Since 10<sup>2</sup> = 100:
<div class="math-block">
log<sub>10</sub> 100 = 2
</div>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h4>Sequences and Series (AP & GP)</h4>
<ul>
<li><strong>Arithmetic Progression (AP) formula:</strong> T<sub>n</sub> = a + (n - 1)d, where <em>a</em> is the first term and <em>d</em> is the common difference.</li>
<li><strong>Geometric Progression (GP) formula:</strong> T<sub>n</sub> = ar<sup>n-1</sup>, where <em>r</em> is the common ratio.</li>
<li><strong>Sample Question:</strong> Find the 10th term of the AP: 3, 7, 11, 15, ...
<ul>
<li><strong>Solution:</strong> Here, the first term <em>a</em> = 3, and the common difference <em>d</em> = 7 - 3 = 4.
<div class="math-block">
T<sub>10</sub> = 3 + (10 - 1) &times; 4 = 3 + 9 &times; 4 = 3 + 36 = 39
</div>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h4>Permutations and Combinations</h4>
<ul>
<li><strong>Permutations (Arrangement - Order matters):</strong> <sup>n</sup>P<sub>r</sub> = n! / (n - r)!</li>
<li><strong>Combinations (Selection - Order does not matter):</strong> <sup>n</sup>C<sub>r</sub> = n! / (r!(n - r)!)</li>
<li><strong>Sample Question:</strong> In how many ways can a committee of 3 members be chosen from a group of 5 candidates?
<ul>
<li><strong>Solution:</strong> Since we are choosing a committee and order does not matter, use the combinations formula:
<div class="math-block">
<sup>5</sup>C<sub>3</sub> = 5! / (3!(5 - 3)!) = (5 &times; 4 &times; 3!) / (3! &times; 2!) = 20 / 2 = 10 ways
</div>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h4>Mental Math Shortcuts</h4>
<ol>
<li><strong>Division by 5:</strong> To divide any number by 5 quickly, multiply the number by 2 and move the decimal point one position to the left.
<ul>
<li><em>Example:</em> 245 / 5 &rarr; (245 &times; 2) = 490 &rarr; 49.0</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Squaring Numbers ending in 5:</strong> To square a two-digit number ending in 5, multiply the first digit by the next highest integer and write "25" at the end.
<ul>
<li><em>Example:</em> 65<sup>2</sup> &rarr; (6 &times; 7) = 42 &rarr; 4,225</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ol>

<h4>Key Historical Figures in Nigeria</h4>
<ul>

<li><strong>Alhaji Abubakar Tafawa Balewa:</strong> The first and only Prime Minister of Nigeria during the First Republic.</li>
<li><strong>Chief Obafemi Awolowo:</strong> Premier of the Western Region, widely recognized for his work in establishing free primary education.</li>
</ul>

<h4>National Symbols and Geography</h4>
<ul>
<li><strong>The National Flag:</strong> Designed in 1959 by Michael Taiwo Akinkunmi, featuring two green stripes representing agriculture and a middle white stripe representing peace.</li>
<li><strong>The Coat of Arms:</strong> Features a black shield representing rich soil, a silver "Y" shape representing the Niger and Benue rivers, and red eagles representing strength.</li>
<li><strong>Geopolitical Zones:</strong> Nigeria is divided into six geopolitical zones, with Lagos State located in the South-West zone.</li>
</ul>

<h4>State Administration</h4>
<ul>
<li><strong>Creation of Lagos State:</strong> Lagos State was created on May 27, 1967, under the military administration of General Yakubu Gowon.</li>
<li><strong>Lagos Governance:</strong> The first military governor of Lagos State was Mobolaji Johnson. The current executive governor is Babajide Sanwo-Olu.</li>
</ul>

<h4>History of the University of Lagos</h4>

<section id="6-the-remote-exam-setup-technical-requirements">
<h2>6. The Remote Exam Setup & Technical Requirements</h2>

<p>For recent admission sessions, the UNILAG Post UTME has been conducted as an online, remotely proctored test. While this saves you a trip to campus, it means you must follow the technical requirements closely.</p>

<h3>System Requirements</h3>
<p>According to the official 2025/2026 and 2026/2027 screening guidelines, candidates must meet specific technical specifications:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Operating System:</strong> The official examination browser used during these cycles was supported on Windows 8.1 or higher, and macOS Ventura or higher.</li>
<li><strong>Hardware:</strong> You must use a laptop computer with a functional, built-in webcam and microphone. Mobile phones are not supported.</li>
<li><strong>Power and Connectivity:</strong> A stable, high-speed internet connection is required. Have a backup power source (such as an inverter or generator) on standby.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Remote Proctoring Guidelines</h3>
<p>To prevent automatic disqualification during the online test, candidates must adhere strictly to these rules:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Keep Your Face Visible:</strong> Do not wear face masks, face caps (fez caps), earpieces, or headphones. Your webcam must remain uncovered throughout the test so proctors can monitor your activity.</li>
<li><strong>Stay in Your Sitting Position:</strong> Once you click the start button, you are not permitted to leave your seat. Your 30 minutes count continuously, even if you log out.</li>
<li><strong>Do Not Use External Hardware:</strong> The use of external cameras or HDMI displays is strictly prohibited. Any attempt to connect them can lead to immediate termination of the test.</li>
<li><strong>Do Not Speak Aloud:</strong> You must not speak aloud or read the questions out during the test.</li>
<li><strong>Do Not Change Tabs:</strong> During the aptitude test, candidates are not permitted to leave the test tab for another tab or application on the computer. Leaving the tab can result in disqualification.</li>
<li><strong>Ensure a Quiet Space:</strong> Nobody is permitted to be around you during the test, not even family members. Proctors monitor background noise and motion, and candidates may be disqualified if anyone is seen or heard near them.</li>
</ul>

<section id="7-step-by-step-unilag-post-utme-registration-guide">
<h2>7. Step-by-Step UNILAG Post UTME Registration Guide</h2>

<p>To register for the screening exercise on the <a href="/unilag-screening-portal">UNILAG screening portal</a>, follow these steps (based on the documented 2026/2027 procedure):</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Log on to the Portal:</strong> Go to the official UNILAG website at <code>unilag.edu.ng</code> and select the Admissions page, or go directly to <code>applications.unilag.edu.ng</code>.</li>
<li><strong>Start a New Application:</strong> Click on "Start New Application" and select "Undergraduate" under the application type.</li>
<li><strong>Log In:</strong> Enter your UTME registration number as your username and your surname in lowercase as your password.</li>
<li><strong>Generate Payment Invoice:</strong> Click on "Generate Payment Advice" to create your invoice.</li>
<li><strong>Pay the Screening Fee:</strong> Take the invoice to any commercial bank to make a payment, or pay online using a debit card. For recent cycles, the total fee was ₦5,500 (comprising a ₦2,000 <a href="/unilag-screening-fee">UNILAG screening fee</a> and a ₦3,500 administrative fee). Note that fees are subject to change.</li>
<li><strong>Complete the Screening Form:</strong> Return to the <a href="/unilag-screening-portal">UNILAG screening portal</a>, log in again, and complete your bio-data, academic details, and subject selection.</li>
<li><strong>Print Your Exam Pass:</strong> Print out your Post-UTME Examination Pass, which will show your scheduled date, time, and instructions for the test.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Common Registration Mistakes to Avoid</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Using an Inactive Email:</strong> Always use a personal, active email address during registration. The admissions office uses this to send examination browser links, technical support updates, and login codes.</li>
<li><strong>Name Discrepancies:</strong> Ensure the spelling of your name matches across your JAMB registration and O'Level documents. Differences in names can cause delays during screening.</li>
<li><strong>Uploading Blurry Results:</strong> Make sure your uploaded O'Level results are clear and readable.</li>
</ul>

<h2>9. Exam-Day Timeline</h2>

<p>Use this timeline to ensure your exam day runs smoothly and you avoid technical issues:</p>

<h3>24 Hours Before the Exam</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Charge All Devices:</strong> Charge your laptop battery fully. Keep a fully charged backup power bank or generator ready in case of power outages.</li>
<li><strong>Test Your Internet Connection:</strong> Run an internet speed test. Ensure you have at least 5GB of active data on your plan.</li>
<li><strong>Prepare Your Laptop:</strong> Close all unnecessary software, background programs, and browser extensions.</li>
</ul>

<h3>2 Hours Before the Exam</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Restart Your Laptop:</strong> Restart your computer to clear any background processes.</li>
<li><strong>Prepare Your Space:</strong> Find a quiet, well-lit room. Clear your desk of any notes or electronic devices.</li>
<li><strong>Adjust Your Lighting:</strong> Make sure your face is clearly lit from the front. Avoid having bright light sources directly behind you.</li>
</ul>

<h3>30 Minutes Before the Exam</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Log In Early:</strong> Open the examination portal and enter your login details to ensure everything is working.</li>
<li><strong>Confirm Your Setup:</strong> Check your webcam feed and microphone connection on the portal.</li>
<li><strong>Secure the Room:</strong> Remind household members not to enter the room or make noise for the next hour.</li>
</ul>

<section id="10-what-happens-after-the-post-utme">
<h2>10. What Happens After the Post UTME?</h2>

<p>Your admission journey does not end when you submit your exam. Knowing the post-exam steps helps you stay prepared:</p>

<ol>
<li><strong>Checking Your Results:</strong> Results are typically released within a few days of the exam. You can check your score by entering your registration details on the results portal.</li>
<li><strong>Calculating Your Aggregate:</strong> Once your screening score is out, use your UTME score, O'Level points, and Post UTME score to calculate your final aggregate out of 100%.</li>
<li><strong>Checking Cut-Off Marks:</strong> The university will release the official departmental cut-off marks for your admission cycle. Compare your aggregate score against these marks to determine your standing.</li>
<li><strong>Monitoring JAMB CAPS:</strong> Check your status regularly on the <a href="/jamb-caps-guide">JAMB CAPS Guide</a>. Once you are offered admission, you must accept it on the portal to secure your place.</li>
<li><strong>Physical Clearance:</strong> Admitted candidates will be invited to the Akoka campus to present original copies of their O'Level results, JAMB admission letter, and birth certificate.</li>
<li><strong>Medical and Drug Testing:</strong> All newly admitted students must undergo a compulsory medical screening and drug test at the university medical center. Testing positive for banned substances can result in your admission being withdrawn.</li>
</ol>

<section id="11-practical-candidate-friendly-tips">
<h2>11. Practical Candidate-Friendly Tips</h2>

<h3>Strategic Time Management</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Skip Difficult Questions:</strong> If you get stuck on a difficult math problem, do not spend several minutes trying to solve it. Mark it, move on, and return to it once you have answered the easier questions.</li>
<li><strong>Pace Yourself:</strong> Aim to spend no more than 30 seconds on each English or General Paper question. This saves valuable calculation time for the math section.</li>
<li><strong>Educated Guessing:</strong> There is no penalty for incorrect answers on the exam. If you are running out of time, do not leave any questions blank. Try to eliminate obviously wrong options and choose the most likely remaining answer.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Managing Exam Anxiety</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Complete Mock Exams:</strong> Practicing under timed conditions before the actual test helps build your confidence and reduces exam-day stress.</li>
<li><strong>Prepare Your Tech in Advance:</strong> Testing your internet connection, laptop, and proctoring software ahead of time helps prevent technical surprises and keeps you calm.</li>
</ul>

<section id="12-common-questions-candidates-forget-to-ask">
<h2>12. Common Questions Candidates Forget to Ask</h2>

<h3>Can I apply for the Post UTME with an awaiting result?</h3>
<p>Awaiting results are not accepted by the time the final admission lists are processed. According to recent guidelines, your complete O'Level results must be uploaded to the official portal before the specified deadline to be considered for admission.</p>

<h3>What should I do if my internet disconnects during the test?</h3>
<p>If your connection drops, try to log back in immediately. The exam system is designed to save your progress, but the 30-minute timer will continue running.</p>

<h3>Can I use an external webcam?</h3>
<p>No. UNILAG's proctoring rules require you to use your laptop’s built-in webcam. External cameras or dual-monitor setups are not permitted.</p>

<h3>Can I change my course after registering for the Post UTME?</h3>
<p>No. Once you submit your registration form on the portal, you cannot change your selected course for that cycle. Make sure your JAMB data and chosen program are correct before completing registration.</p>
