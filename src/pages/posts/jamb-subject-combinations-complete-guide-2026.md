<section id="what-are-they">
  <h2>What are JAMB subject combinations?</h2>
  <blockquote class="def">
    A <b>JAMB subject combination</b> is the set of four subjects a candidate registers for and sits in the UTME to qualify for a specific course. <b>Use of English</b> is compulsory for every candidate; the other three depend on the course.
  </blockquote>
  <p>JAMB publishes the official combination for every course, at every accredited institution, in its brochure — the Integrated Brochure and Syllabus System (IBASS). A Medicine applicant and a Law applicant will register completely different combinations, and the two are not interchangeable. Getting this wrong is one of the most common reasons candidates are disqualified during screening — regardless of how well they scored.</p>
  <div class="callout warn">
    <strong class="label">WHY IT MATTERS</strong>
    A candidate can score 280+ in the UTME and still be disqualified for a course if the subjects sat don't match what that course requires. The subject combination is checked separately from the score, during post-UTME and admission screening.
  </div>
</section>

<section id="how-they-work">
  <h2>How subject combinations work</h2>
  <h3>The four-subject rule</h3>
  <p>Every candidate sits exactly four subjects: <b>Use of English</b>, plus three others determined by the intended course. English is the only subject common to every combination.</p>
  <h3>The JAMB brochure (IBASS) is the final authority</h3>
  <p>The brochure lists the official requirement for every course, at every institution, and is updated between admission cycles. It's available on the JAMB e-Facility portal, at accredited CBT centres, and through the IBASS search tool. If a combination you find elsewhere — including in this guide — conflicts with the brochure, the brochure wins.</p>
  <h3>UTME subjects vs O'Level subjects</h3>
  <p>These are related but separate requirements. Your UTME subjects generally need matching credit passes at O'Level (WAEC, NECO, or NABTEB), and some institutions require O'Level credits — Mathematics is a common example — that don't appear in your UTME combination at all. Check both lists, not just one.</p>
</section>

<section id="choosing">
  <h2>How to choose the right combination</h2>
  <ol class="steps">
    <li><b>Decide your exact course</b> — "Engineering" is too broad; know whether it's Civil, Mechanical, Electrical, or another branch.</li>
    <li><b>Check the JAMB brochure (IBASS)</b> for that specific course, and note any institution-specific remarks.</li>
    <li><b>Cross-check your O'Level results</b> against both the UTME subjects and any additional O'Level-only requirements.</li>
    <li><b>Confirm with your target institution</b> directly, especially for competitive courses like Medicine, Law, and Engineering.</li>
    <li><b>Register carefully</b> and review your subject choices before submitting — corrections after registration are limited and not guaranteed.</li>
  </ol>
</section>

<section id="mistakes">
  <h2>Common mistakes to avoid</h2>
  <h3>Relying on a friend's or sibling's combination</h3>
  <p>Combinations can shift between admission cycles. What worked for someone else two or three years ago is not a substitute for checking the current brochure.</p>
  <h3>Confusing O'Level and UTME requirements</h3>
  <p>A subject you passed at O'Level doesn't automatically belong in your UTME combination, and some institutions require O'Level credits — Mathematics is the most common example — that aren't part of the UTME combination at all.</p>
  <h3>Swapping a required subject for an "easier" one</h3>
  <p>Substituting Agricultural Science for Biology in Medicine, or Government for Literature in Law, is a common and costly error where the substitution isn't officially accepted.</p>
  <h3>Ignoring institution-specific requirements</h3>
  <p>JAMB sets the baseline; individual universities can apply additional or stricter conditions, especially for Medicine, Law, and Engineering. Always check your specific institution's brochure entry.</p>
  <h3>Registering before deciding on a course</h3>
  <p>Choosing subjects first and hoping a course will fit later increases the risk of an eligibility mismatch. Decide the course, then check the required subjects.</p>
</section>

<section id="faq">
  <h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
  <details class="faq">
    <summary>Can I change my subject combination after registration?</summary>
    <p>Only during JAMB's correction-of-data window, for a fee, and it isn't guaranteed. It's safer to get the combination right at registration.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq">
    <summary>Is Use of English compulsory for every course?</summary>
    <p>Yes, with no exceptions. It's the one subject every UTME candidate sits regardless of course.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq">
    <summary>Can I use the same combination for two different courses?</summary>
    <p>Yes, if both courses officially accept the same four subjects — this is common within the same faculty. If the courses sit in different faculties (for example, Medicine and Law), you'll need different combinations and will have to prioritise.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq">
    <summary>Does my subject combination affect my cut-off mark?</summary>
    <p>Not directly — the cut-off is set on your overall score. But if your combination doesn't match the course's requirement, you may not be considered for admission at all, regardless of score.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq">
    <summary>What happens if I register the wrong subjects?</summary>
    <p>Your result may not be valid for your intended course, even above the cut-off mark. Depending on the institution and available capacity, you may be offered a different course that does match your combination.</p>
  </details>
  <details class="faq">
    <summary>Can I offer Further Mathematics as a UTME subject?</summary>
    <p>No — it isn't one of JAMB's standard UTME subject options. A strong O'Level credit in it can still support Mathematics-heavy courses.</p>
  </details>
</section>
